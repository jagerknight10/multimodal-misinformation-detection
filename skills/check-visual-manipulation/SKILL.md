# Check Visual Manipulation

Evaluate whether the image itself is materially manipulated or generated. This is the TRUST-VL visual branch: independent visual understanding followed by separate manipulation and AI-generation checks. During benchmark evaluation, use only the supplied image and pre-retrieved evidence; do not perform or imply live search.

Apply the shared evidence-strength rubric supplied with the routing workflow. Model-based artifact inspection is inherently uncertain; provenance-based comparison should outweigh aesthetic intuition.

## Visual verification procedure

1. **Describe independently.** Before relying on the caption, identify visible people, objects, actions, setting, signage, logos, OCR text, and the apparent photographic style.
2. **Check localized manipulation.** Inspect boundaries, facial regions, hands, object insertion/removal, repeated patches, scale, perspective, occlusion, lighting, shadows, reflections, texture transitions, and inconsistent sharpness.
3. **Check image-wide generation.** Inspect anatomy, object coherence, text rendering, background continuity, repeated patterns, physically implausible detail, and inconsistent photographic noise. Do not treat polished, unusual, or low-quality imagery as generated by default.
4. **Control for benign artifacts.** Explicitly consider compression, rescaling, motion blur, shallow depth of field, screenshots, filters, HDR, stitching, and ordinary editing. These are not material misinformation unless they alter the depicted facts.
5. **Compare provenance.** Use inverse evidence to identify earlier or authoritative versions of the exact image. Compare crop, faces, expression, objects, background, text, and publication context. An exact source image showing a material difference is stronger than visual intuition.
6. **Use direct evidence selectively.** Treat text-led evidence as visual evidence only when it specifically documents the photograph, manipulation, or original source.
7. **Determine the visual result.**
   - `Established failure`: strong provenance evidence or multiple coherent, specific visual anomalies establish a material edit, composite, or generated image.
   - `No established failure`: provenance supports authenticity or no material anomaly survives benign-artifact checks.
   - `Unresolved`: only weak artifact impressions or uncertain provenance exist. This alone is not grounds for `Fake`.

## Classification boundary

Recommend `visual_veracity_distortion` only when the image itself is materially altered or generated and the alteration is central to the deception. An authentic image from the wrong event is cross-modal distortion even if it has been cropped, compressed, color-corrected, or annotated without changing the relevant facts.

## Boundaries

- An authentic image reused with a false caption is normally `cross_modal_consistency_distortion`, not visual manipulation.
- A false caption paired with an otherwise appropriate authentic image is normally `textual_veracity_distortion`.
- Do not treat aesthetic oddness, low resolution, or lack of provenance alone as proof of manipulation.
- Do not infer the benchmark label from filenames, sample IDs, or metadata.

## Specialist output

Return a compact block for the router:

```text
Visual description: <caption-independent scene description>
Manipulation indicators: <specific cues or none>
AI-generation indicators: <specific cues or none>
Provenance comparison: <exact-match evidence and material differences, or unresolved>
Visual result: <Established failure | No established failure | Unresolved>
Visual candidate class: <visual_veracity_distortion | none>
Visual rationale: <one or two evidence-grounded sentences>
```
